Purpose

HRP-405 defines the NASA IRB policy and processes related to determining whether certain types of projects, such as human in the loop (HITL) studies, quality improvements activities, engineering studies, or use of data/specimens are subject to oversight by the NASA Institutional Review Board (IRB). NASA is an official signatory of the Common Rule, codified at 14 CFR part 1230 and applies the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) regulations 21 CFR parts 50, 56, 312, 812 (as applicable) when projects involve activities regulated by the FDA. Prospective review and approval by an IRB is required for all projects that meet the regulatory definitions for research with human subjects (Common Rule) or a clinical investigation (FDA). Activities that do not meet the regulatory definitions are considered “Not Human Subjects Research” (NHSR) for which IRB oversight is not required. Making the determination of whether a project is or is not Human Subjects Research (HSR) is nuanced and context specific. Principal Investigators (PI) are encouraged to consult with NASA IRB staff whenever there is uncertainty.

Policy 1. Official NHSR determination Authority. a. Only the NASA IRB Staff, the NASA IRB Chair, or the convened NASA IRB are authorized to make an official IRB determination that an activity does or does not involve human subjects research (i.e., Not Human Subjects Research (NHSR)). While the federal regulations do not mandate that researchers obtain official NHSR documentation from the IRB, other NASA review boards (e.g., TRR, TSS, Safety) may require formal documentation. 1. NASA IRB’s determination that an activity is human subjects research supersedes the determination of another institution or agency’s IRB when NASA is engaged. 2. NASA IRB may make an NHSR determination based on information provided in a full-board/expedited/exempt submission.

3. The NASA IRB does not have authority to grant retroactive approval for a project meeting the definitions of research with human subjects. 4. When needed, the official NHSR determination should be sought prior to initiation. If a project is determined to constitute Human Subjects Research (HSR), prospective review and approval by the IRB must be sought before research activities begin. 5. NASA IRB has the authority to over-rule a researcher’s self- determination of NHSR. b. Principal Investigators (PI) may request EDRO documentation, rather than official IRB documentation, that a proposed activity is Not Human Subjects Research (NHSR) provided the activity: 1. falls entirely outside of NASA and U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) Common Rule regulatory definitions of Research with Human Subjects, and Food and Drug Administration (FDA) definition for clinical investigation; and 2. does not involve International Partner (IP) research, reviewed by another international ethics board; and i. IP research is assessed for a NHSR determination case-by-case and may deviate from this policy as determined by the NASA IRB Chair 2. An NHSR determination applies only to IRB oversight and does not absolve requirements from other offices or boards (e.g., Safety Office, Crew Office, LSAH/LSDA) and/or requirements from NASA Office of General Counsel. Fulfillment of any additional requirements are the responsibility of the PI. a. For projects involving astronaut subjects the PI must coordinate with the NASA Crew Office regardless of an NHSR determination from the NASA IRB. b. Projects involving International Partners (IP) continue to require review by the HRMRB and other international ethics boards as appropriate. 3. PI continue to be responsible for: a. Compliance with all applicable federal and state laws. b. Ethical collection and use of information used in the project. c. Adherence to applicable NASA policies. 4. An NHSR determination applies only to the activities as initially described. A new determination should be made prior to implementation of changes.

Procedures

1. Principal Investigator (PI) initial assessment

a. PI should review the definitions and examples in this document and guidance HRP-415 to determine whether an activity meets the regulatory definitions for human subjects research. b. PI may complete the ‘Not Human Subjects Research (NHSR) Questionnaire’ (Appendix 1) to assess their planned activities. PI are encouraged to consult with NASA IRB staff whenever there is uncertainty. i) NASA IRB staff may advise PI informally by email or telephone; and/or ii) NASA IRB staff may request submission of a ‘Not Human Subjects Research (NHSR) Questionnaire’ (Appendix 1) through the eIRB to initiate a formal IRB determination process. iii) NASA IRB Staff, the NASA IRB Chair, or the convened NASA IRB may determine that the project constitutes Human Subjects Research (HSR) for which IRB oversight is required. The NASA IRB staff may request submission of a protocol and supporting documents through the eIRB for NASA IRB review (Exempt, Expedited, or Full Board). iv) PI are encouraged to retain documentation of the relevant factors impacting initial assessments of NHSR (e.g., retention of a completed NHSR Questionnaire within the project records). 2. EDRO review and documentation a. Projects meeting criteria for EDRO review (Policy 1.b above) should follow EDRO procedures for requesting EDRO documentation. 3. NASA IRB NHSR determination a. PI may initiate an official determination made by the NASA IRB staff through submission of Appendix 1: ‘Not Human Subjects (NHSR) Questionnaire’ b. NASA IRB staff review of the NHSR Questionnaire occurs within the eIRB system. c. PI initiates the formal NHSR review in the eIRB via “Create New Study” process. i) Basic study information (i.e., title, brief description) is entered in the eIRB smartform. ii) The NHSR Questionnaire is attached in place of the protocol document. iii) PIs do not need to list study team members or upload COI/CITI documentation for an NHSR determination iv) Informed consent and recruitment materials are not required for an NHSR determination. If consent or recruitment materials are submitted, they should be attached to the Local Site Documents section of the eIRB smartfom. v) Data collection materials (e.g., surveys, questionnaires) are not typically required as part of an NHSR submission but may be requested by IRB staff when making their determination. These materials may be attached to the Local Site Documents section of the eIRB smartform. vi) Documentation from Life Sciences Data Archive (LSDA) or Lifetime Surveillance of Astronaut Health (LSAH) that data can be provided to the PI de-identified/non-attributable must be attached for projects involving secondary data obtained from LSDA or LSAH. (1) If LSDA/LSAH cannot provide data in a de-identified/non-attributable format, the project is not eligible for NHSR review.

d. The NASA IRB Chair/Designee reviews the NHSR Questionnaire and determines whether the research meets the definitions for research with human subjects. i) The form may be returned to the PI for clarifications or to request additional information. ii) PI of projects determined to meet the definitions of HSR (i.e., NOT NHSR) are directed to submit a research protocol and supporting documents (e.g., consent forms, recruitment materials, surveys, questionnaires, etc.) to the eIRB for applicable review by the NASA IRB (Exempt, Expedited, or Full Board). iii) Projects confirmed as not meeting the definitions for Research with Human Subjects (i.e., NHSR) receive formal documentation in the form of a letter emailed through the eIRB system. e. International regulations related to human subjects research may vary. Projects determined not to meet the U.S. regulatory definitions for research with human subjects may still be considered HSR subject to oversight by other International Partner (IP) agencies. i) All projects conducted on ISS must also be reviewed by the Human Research Multilateral Review Board (HRMRB). ii) HRMRB review is initiated after an IRB determination letter is issued. iii) Responsibility for obtaining approval from other IP agencies. (1) NASA HRP funded studies – ROI facilitates approval from other IP agencies. (2) Studies that are not funded by HRP – IP approval is the responsibility of the PI 4. Informed Consent a. Domestic NHSR projects, conducted by U.S. PI, involving U.S. subjects only: i) U.S. regulations governing human subjects do not require an informed consent process for NHSR; however, best practice for ethical work with human participants includes informing them of the project’s purpose, what they are asked to do, expected duration, associated risks, etc. of their participation. ii) PI are responsible for ensuring that any non-IRB related consent/ permission/agreement(s) are obtained and documented as required. b. NHSR projects involving international partner (IP) as PI or subjects and/or projects conducted on ISS that intend to enroll US astronauts should add the following language to the Multilateral (HRMRB) Consent Form: “NOTE FOR US CREW ONLY: The NASA IRB has determined the proposed project is not considered human subjects research per U.S. 14CFR1230. As such, 14CFR1230 regulations have not been applied and the project is not under the purview of the NASA IRB.”

Definitions

Applicable to all activities: 2018 Common Rule [14 CFR 1230.102, 45 CFR 46.102] Generalizable Knowledge – Activities designed to develop or contribute to generalizable knowledge are those designed to draw broadly applicable conclusions or generalize findings SOP - 405: Human Subjects Research Determination v.7.1 Page |4

beyond a single individual or internal program, uncover underlying principles that have predictive value and can be applied to other circumstances, or develop or test scientific theories or hypotheses. Human Subject – a living individual about whom and investigator conducting research [14 CFR1230.102(e)(1)] i) obtains information or biospecimens through intervention or interaction with the individual and uses, studies, or analyzes the information or biospecimens; or ii) obtains, uses, studies, analyzes, or generates identifiable private information or biospecimens. Identifiable private information – private information for which the identity of the subject is or may be readily ascertained (directly or indirectly), by the investigator (or others) or associated with the information. [14 CFR1230.102(e)(5)] Interaction – communication or interpersonal contact between investigator and subject. [14 CFR1230.102(e)(3)] Intervention – includes both physical procedures by which information or biospecimens are gathered and manipulations of the subject or the subject’s environment that are performed for research purposes. [14 CFR1230.102(e)(2)] Private information – information about behavior that occurs in a context in which an individual can reasonably expect that no observation or recording is taking place, as well as information that has been provided for specific purposes by an individual and that the individual can reasonably expect will not be made public. [14 CFR1230.102(e)(4)] Research – a systematic investigation, including research development, testing, and evaluation, designed to develop or contribute to generalizable knowledge. [14 CFR1230.102(l)] Systematic investigation – Examination that involves a prospective plan that incorporates data collection, either quantitative or qualitative, and data analysis to answer a question.

In addition to the above definitions, activities regulated by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) also apply the FDA definitions [21 CFR 50.3] Clinical Investigation -Any experiment that involves a test article and one or more human subjects that is either subject to requirements for prior submission to the Food and Drug Administration under section 505(i), or 520(g) of the act, or is not subject to requirements for prior submission to the Food and Drug Administration under these sections of the act, but the results of which are intended to be submitted to, or held for inspection by the Food and Drug Administration as part of an application for a research or marketing permit.” The term does not include experiments that are subject to the provision of 21 CFR 58, regarding nonclinical laboratory studies. [21 CFR 50.3(c)] Note: Under FDA regulations, the terms “research” and “clinical investigation” are synonymous. Test Article - A test article means any drug (including a biological product for human use), medical device for human use, human food additive, color additive, electronic product, or any other article subject to regulation under the Federal Food, Drug and Cosmetic Act [21 CFR 50.3(j)].
