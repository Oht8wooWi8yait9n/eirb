Office of Research Assurance Human Subject Research Protections Standard Operating Procedures (SOP)

SOP- 810: Mandated Reporting

Number Effective Date Version Number Previous Versions 810 8/7/2024 v.1 n/a

Purpose SOP-810 describes the process to ensure prompt reporting by the NASA Institutional Review Board (IRB) to appropriate institutional officials, sponsors, coordinating centers, and the appropriate regulatory heads.

Policy 1. The NASA IRB requires compliance with all applicable federal reporting requirements in the conduct of human subjects research. The NASA IRB notifies the appropriate officials when research falls under the purview of a federal regulatory agency and one or more of the following occur: a. Unanticipated problems involving risks to participants or others; b. Serious or continuing noncompliance with the regulations or IRB requirements; c. Suspension or termination of IRB approval fore research due to noncompliance 2. Reporting to regulatory federal agencies is not required if the research voluntarily closes a project to new participant accrual or temporarily halts research procedures. The NASA IRB may recommend voluntary closure to the Principal Investigator (PI). 3. Expiration of IRB approval is not reportable under this SOP, unless repeated and constitutes serious and/or continuing noncompliance. 4. Researchers may have mandated reporting responsibilities outside of the NASA IRB.

Procedures 1. The NASA IRB prepares a report within 15 calendar days from the date the IRB conducts a final review of an unanticipated problem involving risk to participants or others, serious or continuing noncompliance, or suspension or termination of research. The report must include: a. Study title b. Study number (grant identifier)

c. Summary of the nature of the event d. IRB findings e. Corrective actions taken/recommended The NASA IRB Chair or designee approves the report and forwards to the NASA Office of Research Assurance, Institutional Official, appropriate federal agency, and any other parties deemed necessary. 2. For FDA regulated research, the PI must report to the sponsor, who will report findings to the FDA, copying the NASA IRB. If the PI is the sponsor, then the PI will report to the FDA. The NASA IRB reserves the right to prepare a report directly to the FDA. 3. If the Department of Defense (DoD) supports the research, the report is sent to the DoD Human Research Protection Officer (HRPO) within 30 days. 4. For projects that do not have federal funding, reports will be send to the IO or designee. 5. Reports must be provided to the relevant federal agency within 30 days of event discovery. 6. NASA IRB staff will place reports in the IRB file system.
