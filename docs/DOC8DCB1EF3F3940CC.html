Office of Research Assurance Human Subject Research Protections Standard Operating Procedures (SOP)

SOP- 720: : Return of Research Results

SOP-720 defines the NASA IRB policy and processes with respect to planning for and handling the return of research results to individual study participants. Please note this SOP does not change any other NASA medical policies or procedures for medical treatment or care of crewmembers.

Individual research results are information linked to the identity of a research participant, that both pertains to that research participant and is the focus of the research study. Examples include an individual’s ability to withstand a given gravitational force and still perform a task, psychological findings, or the disclosure of the study arm to which a participant was randomized. Individual research results can be clinical/medical or social behavioral.

Individual research results are broken down into three categories, which are based on when the information was created: 1. Baseline findings – results collected at the beginning of the individual’s participation to determine if the participant qualifies for the research (screening to determine inclusion/exclusion criteria) 2. In-study findings – results generated during the progress of the research 3. End-of-study findings – results collected, collated, and/or interpreted after the study has ended and data analysis for all study participants is complete

The return of individual research results should be viewed as the “offering” of individual research results. Participants should be given the opportunity to decide whether or not to receive the results. Legal reporting requirements, such as mandated reporting of communicable diseases, abuses, and intent to harm self or others are an exception.

respect for persons and beneficence. In regard to respect for persons, researchers have a duty toward research participants that includes identifying, verifying, and communicating individual research results of clinical significance, even though researchers are not serving in a clinical capacity during research. Disclosure of individual research results also builds a sense of partnership in the research process, leading to greater public trust and willingness to participate in research. Beneficence provides a directive to avoid harm to participants. To the extent that the return of individual research results to participants could lead to harm or discomfort, whether physical, psychological, financial, reputational, or social, particularly with no offset of direct benefit, there must be a consideration of whether it is appropriate to return such findings. Although the disclosure of individual research results may prove valuable to participants and their families in regard to their health or welfare, participants may misuse or misunderstand individual research results provided to them and take inappropriate medical or personal action. Participants may also misconstrue the purpose of the research, failing to distinguish between research and clinical care. Lastly, the maintenance of participant contact information increases risks to participants for data breaches and unauthorized disclosure.

It is possible for research to be conducted respectfully without the return of individual research results. If participants are made aware during the informed consent process that individual research results will not be returned, and they choose to participate in the study, their autonomy has been respected. Evaluation of the benefit of returning individual research results should also consider: • The potential to cause bias in the study (mainly with respect to timing of disclosure) • Whether the meaning and significance of the research results are known • If results have low importance or insignificance • Lack of validity • Lack of actionability • Administration and/or financial burden to the investigator, institution, and other parties

Additionally, in some studies, it may be impossible to provide individual research results because the participants are de-identified or anonymous (e.g., certain tissue banking activities, research conducted under a waiver of informed consent, exempt studies, and cluster randomized studies). IRBs must consider the risks and benefits of disclosing or returning individual research results during the design, conduct, and oversight of research acknowledging the rebuttals but presuming disclosure. The return of individual research results is permissible, but not mandatory and the decision to disclose research results to individual participants will be made on a case-by-case basis. When determining whether to return individual research results, the more likely the results are validated and actionable, the more justification for disclosing (returning) them to participants. Actionable can mean either clinically actionable or personally actionable. Validated usually means the test or diagnosis is conducted using devices or assays that have Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval or certification under Clinical Laboratory

Policy

Regardless of the return of individual research results happening at baseline, in-study, or end-of-study: 1. For studies involving NASA astronauts as participants, the Protocol Compliance Officer or Alternate Compliance Officer, in consultation with the NASA IRB if deemed necessary, will determine if individual research results are actionable and/or validated. For studies not involving NASA astronauts as participants, the NASA IRB Chair/designee, in consultation with the NASA IRB or any other experts if deemed necessary, will determine if individual research results are actionable and/or validated. 2. For studies involving NASA astronauts as participants, the Protocol Compliance Officer or Alternate Compliance Officer, in consultation with the NASA IRB if deemed necessary, will determine if individual research results reveal a condition or

When warranted, individual research results will be returned to participants, or their legally authorized representative, directly (this may be in oral or written form). 5. For studies involving NASA astronauts as participants, the Protocol Compliance Officer or Alternate Compliance Officer will disclose (return) or is to be involved in the disclosure (return) of individual research results. Individual research results that are determined by the Protocol Compliance Officer or alternate to be a real or potential risk to the mission will require timely notification to the NASA astronaut and his/she/their assigned flight surgeon. After this, if necessary, individual research results may be placed in the participant’s medical record. 6. For studies not involving NASA astronauts as participants, where possible, an individual with a clinical relationship with the research participant will disclose (return) or is to be involved with the disclosure (return) of individual research results. Individual research results might also be provided to the participant’s health care provider or be placed in his/her/their medical record. 7. Principal Investigators (PIs) are not to disclose individual research results to participants unless otherwise deemed acceptable or necessary by the NASA IRB. 8. If a researcher has an ethical objection to allowing participants to opt out of receiving clinically significant, actionable, validated, lifesaving individual research results, he/she/they need not enroll these individuals in the research study and they must outline this exclusion criterion in the protocol and informed consent form. 9. If a researcher discovers a clinically significant, actionable, validated, lifesaving individual research result for a participant who has opted out of receiving individual research results, the investigator should seek advice from the NASA IRB. Responsibilities

Principal Investigator 1. Whether or not the researcher is also the treating physician, if the researcher or a member of the research team identifies individual research results of potential concern, it is the PI’s duty to handle these findings responsibly and promptly. 2. At the time of initial IRB review: a. Researchers are encouraged to consult with an expert (e.g., radiologist, clinical geneticist, etc.) to determine which potential individual research results may be of clinical significance during protocol development. b. The researcher must describe the return of individual research results in the protocol and consent form. i. The protocol must outline: 1. The basic information regarding the program for the release of data, including what information will be released, who will do so, and when the disclosure is expected to take place; and 2. The process for keeping participant contact information readily available after the study is finished, specific if, how, and for how long such information is to be retained ii. The informed consent form must include: 1. The intent to provide individual research results; 2. The basic information regarding the program for the return of results, including a basic description of the information to be returned, who will provide the information, and when the disclosure will take place; 3. The potential risks/benefits of disclosure;
