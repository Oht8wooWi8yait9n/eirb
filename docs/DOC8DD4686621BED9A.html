Exempt Research Protocol

Federal regulations designate certain types of research involving humans as exempt from many of the regulatory requirements governing human subjects research. Accordingly, exempt research is subject to fewer IRB review requirements. PI may use this abbreviated protocol document to submit exempt research for review by the NASA IRB.

Eligibility for Exempt review:

  • Research presents little to no risk of harm to participants (i.e., minimal risk); and
  • All research procedures fit into one or more of the regulatory exemption categories (e.g., normal education practices, surveys/interviews with adults, benign behavioral interventions, secondary data use when recorded de-identified, taste-testing of wholesome foods) [SOP – HRP-420]

Section G: Participant Privacy and Data Confidentiality

Privacy concerns people, whereas confidentiality concerns data. Specifically, confidentiality refers to the researcher’s plan to handle, manage and disseminate the participant’s identifiable private information. Privacy refers to a person’s wish to control the access of others to themselves.

Anonymous data: Unidentified (i.e., personally identifiable information was not collected, or if collected, identifiers were not retained and cannot be retrieved); information or materials (e.g., data or biospecimens) that cannot be linked directly or indirectly by anyone to their source(s).

Coded: Direct personal identifiers have been removed (e.g., from data or biospecimens) and replaced with words, letters or numbers; but the original identifiers are retained and can be traced back to the source by someone with the code or key.

De-identified: All direct personal identifiers are permanently removed, no code or key exists to link the information or materials to their original source, and the remaining information cannot reasonably be used by anyone to identify the source.

Section H: Process of Consent

Informed consent is a key aspect of ethical human subjects research. This ethical obligation exists for all research – exempt and non-exempt. Research meeting the criteria for exemption is not subject to the formal consent requirements established in the federal regulations, allowing flexibility in how PIs obtain informed consent.

While use of a formal informed consent form containing all “elements of consent” is not required for research under exempt review; the NASA IRB expects that researchers provide prospective participants information about the research and confirm their agreement. Signed documentation is not required and in many cases the process and documents can be simple and streamlined. The NASA IRB has an Exempt Consent Template that PIs may use when designing a consent process for exempt research, however, PIs have flexibility in how information about the study is shared.

At minimum, the information should include:

  • A statement that the project involves research;
  • A general description of procedures and time commitment;
  • Any potential risk/discomfort related to participation (e.g., discomfort responding to sensitive or personal questions, privacy concerns, disclosure risks);
  • Indication that participation is voluntary and that they may skip any questions in an interview or survey that they are not comfortable answering;
  • Privacy and confidentiality protections;
  • An outline of plans for data-sharing of future research use of information.

When applicable, consent should also include:

  • Any plans to obtain information about participants from records (e.g., medical information, data from prior study);
  • Plans for audio/video recording or photographs and how the recordings/images will be used and retained;
  • Any plans for capturing information via screen-recording, key-stroke-logging, etc.:
  • If deception about the purpose or nature of the study is planned, prospect participants must be informed that they will be unaware of, or misled about, the nature or purposes of the research, and agree to proceed.
